Seven rules

Each one traces to the cover thesis or to §10, and each is falsifiable — you can look at a screen and say whether it holds. The failure mode the brief warns about is designing screens you never build, so the effort goes into rules, which survive into code.

1 · The baseline is always on screen

No number appears alone. Every metric sits next to what it was, and every chart draws a reference line for the user's own four-week baseline. The chartBaseline token exists for exactly one purpose: drawing "you, four weeks ago."

Test: point at any number. Can you tell if it's better or worse than the user's own past, without tapping?

2 · Quiet by default, loud once a week

Attention is a budget, spent almost entirely on the weekly rank-up. Saturated colour is rationed to the tier metals. Motion over 0.3s is rationed to one reveal. 56pt type appears once per app.

Test: screenshot Today and Rank side by side. Is Rank obviously the more important one, without reading a word?

3 · Ranges, not points

Calorie targets are bands. Photo estimates are ranges with confidence. Weight is a 7-day average, because raw daily weight is mostly water and glycogen — showing it as the headline is noise presented as signal.

Test: is there a single precise number anywhere the underlying data can't support one?

4 · Regression is a reset, never a failure

There is deliberately no error colour for logged intake. Going above your band is not an error, it's Tuesday. A designer reaching for "the red one" won't find it — the palette offers positive, safety, critical, and nothing in between.

Enforced: no_punishment_framing, severity error.

5 · "No data" is a designed state

iOS won't tell you whether HealthKit access was denied — an empty result is indistinguishable from "no data." So both are worded identically, and neither reads as broken. Four states per surface, from the first commit.

Test: does any empty state use a warning icon or the word "failed"?

6 · Three tabs. Two taps to log.

Today · Log · Rank. A repeat food logs on tap at its last portion with an undo toast, which is what buys the 2-tap number — the measurable claim against the incumbents.

Test: cold open to logged meal. Count the taps. Write it down next to MyFitnessPal's.

7 · The engine is the product

The UI's job is to make the scoring legible, not to decorate it. The rank screen always answers: what's my tier, what moved it, and what's the cheapest thing that would move it next.

Test: show it to a stranger. Can they say what would raise their rank?

Anti-patterns

Red "over budget". Streak-break animations. Confetti on weight loss — it rewards rate, which the engine deliberately caps. Absolute leaderboards. Raw daily weight as the headline. Daily nudges. A fourth tab.

Each has a reason, not just a preference. See 00-design-principles.md.

Colour, with a budget

The contrast ratios below are computed on this page, live, against the current theme — not typed in by hand. generate.py runs the same check in CI and fails the build on a regression. It has already caught one: the first tertiary text colour passed on canvas and failed on cards at 4.43:1.

Neutrals & text

Accent & semantic

There is no error token, and that is the design. positive means "in your band". safety is reserved for the six §10 interventions — calorie floor, goal-weight BMI, rate ceiling — and is named "safety" rather than "warning" so its scope can't creep. critical is destructive confirmation only. Nothing in the palette can express "you ate too much," because the product does not have that thought.

The ladder is a count, not a colour

Any honest rendering of "silver" and "platinum" as metals gives you two light cool greys. And roughly 1 in 12 men have some colour vision deficiency, which makes a hue-only ladder unreadable for them. So tier is encoded in the number of notches cut into the badge, with metal colour as the redundant second signal. Both derive from one Tier enum, so they can never disagree.

The name earns its keep. A notch is a mark you cut to record progress — a tally. It's physical, humble and pre-digital, and it means one more than before rather than a score. That's the thesis in a single image: you're not being measured against a scale, you're accumulating marks against your own past. The same primitive carries the consistency ring below — seven notches, one per day.

Type scale

Every style binds to an iOS text style so Dynamic Type works without extra code — required, not optional, in a health app. Metric styles use tabular figures so digits don't jitter as a value updates in place.

1

The badge carries the count, not just the metal

Three notches for Gold. At tab-bar size the silhouette still reads as three, which is what makes the ladder legible to someone who can't separate gold from silver by hue. VoiceOver hears "Gold. Tier 3 of 5."

2

"Up from Silver" — and, on a bad week, "Reset to Silver"

Never dropped, demoted, or lost. All three are in the linter's banned-phrase list at error severity. The reset copy always states the way back: "Gold is four consistent days away."

3

The weights are on screen

0.50 / 0.25 / 0.25, shown rather than hidden. The decision log accepts that consistency-weighting will feel unresponsive to someone chasing fast results, and specifies mitigating through copy, not by reweighting. Showing the weights is that mitigation — an unresponsive rank becomes a legible one.

4

Next step, always present

The one question the screen must never leave unanswered: what's the cheapest thing that would move this? A rank you can't act on is a rank that feels arbitrary.

5

What isn't here

No confetti — it would reward rate, which the engine deliberately caps for safety. No other user's name or number anywhere. No percentile. Your rank moves only for reasons you caused; that's the thesis, enforced by the screen.

The rewrite rule

Every string describing a shortfall must answer "what now?" rather than "what went wrong?" State the fact neutrally, then give the next action. "Above your band" is a fact; "over budget" is a judgement that frames food as money you've spent.

Seventeen phrases are banned by the linter at error severity — you failed, lost your streak, over budget, demoted, dropped to and the rest. §10 asks you to review every string against the no-punishment rule; a lint rule reviews them on every commit instead of once, in a hurry, the week before TestFlight.
